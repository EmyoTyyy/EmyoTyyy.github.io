Six lines, and you can already read one.

A horizontal bar is a function. A vertical line dropping from it is the variable it binds. A bar underneath joins a function to its argument. That is the entire notation, and it is enough to draw every program that has ever been written.

  • Identity

    λx. x

    Takes a thing, hands it straight back.

  • Constant

    λx y. x

    Takes two, forgets the second. Also true.

  • Substitution

    λx y z. x z (y z)

    With K, enough to express anything computable.

  • Fixed point

    λf. (λx. f (x x)) (λx. f (x x))

    How a language with no names writes a loop.

  • Omega

    (λx. x x) (λx. x x)

    Reduces to itself. Runs forever, on purpose.

  • Three

    λf x. f (f (f x))

    A number, written as "do it three times".

The big one

Tromp Diagrams

A whole site for looking at lambda calculus instead of only reading about it. Type an expression, watch it become a picture, then watch the picture reduce. Twelve pages of it, in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript: no build step, no package manager, no backend, and no dependency on anything outside the folder.

Open the site Source

Visualizer

The main tool. Several β-reduction strategies side by side in multiple panes, your own definitions, shareable URLs, and export to PNG or WebM so a reduction can leave the page as a video.

Play

You get the diagram. You guess the expression. Hints, scoring, a daily puzzle.

Tree

Several strategies advanced in parallel, so you can see where they branch and where they meet again.

Encodings, combinators, halting, history

Reference pages that carry their weight: an encoding lab for naturals, booleans, pairs and both Church and Scott lists, a combinator zoo with diagrams, a step-by-step walk through the halting problem, and where all of it came from.

Learn

Guided lessons from variables and abstraction up through booleans, pairs, numerals and lists.

God mode

A headless reducer that skips the drawing entirely and just runs, for terms with no patience for animation.

Achievements

An unlockable badge grid, including a gold tier for things that are genuinely hard to do.

Let me ask my best friend Claude.

I say this a lot. Often enough that it has become a running joke, and honestly it is not much of a joke, because it is what I actually do.

Most of what is on this page was built in conversation. That is not a shortcut, or at least not the kind people mean. The parser still has to parse. The reducer still has to be correct on capture-avoiding substitution or the diagrams quietly lie. Nobody else is around at two in the morning when something breaks, so I still have to understand every line well enough to fix it.

What changed is how quickly a question turns into a thing you can click on. That has turned out to matter more than I expected.

Sheaf

Electron · TypeScript

A local-first LaTeX editor. Your projects stay as ordinary files on your disk and compile with the TeX distribution already on your machine. Errors land on the line you actually wrote, even from a file three includes deep.
